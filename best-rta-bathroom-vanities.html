Frequently Asked Questions

What does RTA mean for a bathroom vanity?

RTA stands for ready-to-assemble. The cabinet ships flat in panels along with the hardware and instructions, and you build it yourself rather than receiving a fully assembled unit. Every RTA bathroom vanity in this guide includes a sink top, pre-cut faucet holes, and the fasteners needed for assembly, so the only tools you typically need are a screwdriver and the included Allen key.

Are RTA bathroom vanities as sturdy as pre-assembled ones?

It depends on the hardware and material. A well-built RTA vanity like the ones in this guide uses cam-lock and dowel joinery that holds as tight as a factory-assembled cabinet once it is squared and leveled against the wall. Weaker flat-pack cabinets rely on screws driven straight into particleboard, which strip out faster. Checking for cam-lock hardware and soft-close hinges is the fastest way to separate a durable RTA vanity from a flimsy one.

How long does it take to assemble an RTA bathroom vanity?

Most of the vanities in this guide take 45 minutes to just over an hour with a screwdriver and the included hardware, according to owner reviews. Solid-wood models like the CozyHommie and HCIOAN cabinets take longer, since the panels are heavier and benefit from a second person holding pieces square while you drive screws.

Do RTA bathroom vanities come with a faucet?

No. None of the vanities in this guide include a faucet, though all of them ship with the sink top's faucet holes pre-drilled to a standard configuration. Budget an extra $30 to $80 for a faucet that matches your chosen vanity's hole spacing, and check whether it's a single-hole or widespread configuration before you buy.

What size RTA bathroom vanity fits a small bathroom?

For a small full bathroom or powder room, a 27 to 30-inch vanity like the HCIOAN 27-inch or Mirightone 30-inch fits without crowding the door swing. Measure your available wall width and subtract a couple of inches of clearance on each side before picking a size, and check the cabinet's depth too, since anything deeper than 21 inches needs more floor space than a standard layout allows.
