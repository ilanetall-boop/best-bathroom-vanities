Things to Know Before You Buy

  • Pre-assembled still means some setup. The cabinet arrives built, but you'll still level it, anchor it to wall studs, and connect the water supply and drain lines yourself or hire a plumber for an hour of work.
  • Measure the doorway width too. A 54-inch vanity cabinet can be too wide to carry through a standard 32-inch interior door without tilting it on its side.
  • MDF dominates this price range. Six of the seven picks below use an MDF or engineered wood cabinet. That's normal at this price point, and the moisture resistance depends more on the finish and edge sealing than the core material itself.
  • Countertop and sink are sometimes bundled, sometimes not. Check each listing before you buy, since some pre-assembled bathroom vanities include the sink and top and others price the cabinet alone.
  • Confirm your rough-in plumbing matches. Moving a drain or supply line adds cost and time that no vanity, pre-assembled or otherwise, can save you.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does "pre-assembled" actually mean for a bathroom vanity?

A pre-assembled bathroom vanity arrives with the cabinet frame, doors, and drawers already built and attached. You still need to secure it to the wall, connect the plumbing, and in most cases attach the countertop and sink separately, but you skip the panel-by-panel construction that ready-to-assemble (RTA) kits require.

How long does it take to install a pre-assembled vanity?

Most of the pre-assembled bathroom vanities in this guide go in within two to four hours once the box is open, including leveling the cabinet, anchoring it to studs, and hooking up the supply lines and drain. That timeline assumes your existing plumbing rough-in matches the new vanity's footprint. If you're moving a drain or widening the cabinet, budget extra time for a plumber.

Are pre-assembled vanities worth the extra cost over RTA kits?

If your time is worth more than a weekend of cabinet-building, yes. Pre-assembled bathroom vanities cost more per inch than flat-pack RTA kits because you're paying someone else's factory labor instead of your own. For a first bathroom remodel or a rental turnover on a deadline, that trade usually pays for itself.
