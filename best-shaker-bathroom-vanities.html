Things to Know Before You Buy

  • "Shaker" describes the door, not the material. Every one of the best shaker bathroom vanities here uses a flat door with a recessed center panel, but the cabinet underneath is usually MDF or engineered wood rather than solid hardwood, even when a listing name says "solid wood."
  • RTA and Unassembled are not identical. The RTA Quicklock cabinets ship with most joinery pre-cut, while the Design House Brookings is sold fully unassembled and needs more build time.
  • "No Back" cabinets need a plumbing check first. Two vanities in this comparison, the 60-inch and 42-inch Quicklock models, skip a solid rear panel so the cabinet fits over existing wall plumbing, which only works if your rough-in is already positioned for it.
  • Sizes span 24 to 60 inches. The eight shaker vanities we compared range from a 24-inch powder-room cabinet to a 60-inch double-sink-width unit, at prices from $268.63 to $519.99.
  • None of these listings publish a review count. Weigh published dimensions, material, and price more heavily than star ratings alone when you compare these vanities.

Frequently Asked Questions

What makes a bathroom vanity shaker style?
A shaker vanity uses flat cabinet doors with a recessed center panel and a simple, square-edged frame, instead of the raised panels or ornate molding you see on traditional cabinets. Every one of the best shaker bathroom vanities in this comparison keeps that plain, symmetrical door front, which is why the style works in farmhouse, modern, and transitional bathrooms alike.
How much does a shaker bathroom vanity cost?
Prices in this comparison range from $268.63 for the Design House Brookings, a 33-inch unassembled cabinet, up to $519.99 for the 60-inch Quicklock RTA cabinet built for double-sink counters. Most single-sink shaker vanities between 24 and 36 inches wide land between $270 and $450, with price tracking width more closely than any other spec.
Are RTA shaker vanities hard to install?
RTA, or ready-to-assemble, cabinets ship flat and need to be screwed together before installation, while a listing marked Unassembled, like the Design House Brookings, typically involves more assembly steps than an RTA kit. Cabinets marked No Back in this comparison skip a solid rear panel so the cabinet can sit over existing wall plumbing, which simplifies installation but means you should confirm your plumbing rough-in lines up before you order.
