What We Liked

The clearest strength in this merax bathroom vanity review is counter space. A single 48 inch basin leaves more open surface on either side of the sink than a vanity split into two smaller basins at the same width, which is useful if you keep toiletries, a soap dispenser, or a tray on the counter instead of tucking everything into the cabinet below.

Pricing works in the Merax's favor depending on what you line it up against. At $599.00, it costs only $4 more than Tennant Brand's 24 inch Felix, despite offering roughly double the counter and cabinet width. For a primary bathroom that needs real storage and surface area rather than a compact footprint, that gap in value helps the Merax even though it's the priciest option in this specific comparison.

Closed cabinet storage is the other detail buyers researching this merax bathroom vanity review tend to bring up. A single door or set of doors underneath the basin keeps towels, cleaning supplies, and toiletries out of sight, which some owners prefer over open shelving that shows clutter from across the room.

What Could Be Better

The clearest drawback in this merax bathroom vanity review is price relative to its closest match. eclife's 48 inch double-sink vanity covers the identical width for $549.99, and eclife's single-sink 48 inch model matches the Merax's one-basin layout for $569.99. Both undercut the Merax by $30 to $50 while occupying the same footprint, so the Merax has to earn that premium through fit, finish, or brand preference rather than raw specs.

Material is the second tradeoff. Merax builds the cabinet from MDF, or engineered wood, rather than solid wood, a choice that's standard at this price point across the entire comparison set but still leaves the cabinet more sensitive to standing water around the base than hardwood construction would be. Buyers who mop frequently around the vanity's base should keep that in mind regardless of which of these four vanities they choose.

Who Is It For?

This merax bathroom vanity review points toward one clear buyer: someone furnishing a primary bathroom who wants a single wide basin instead of his-and-hers sinks. If you live alone, share a bathroom in shifts rather than at the same time, or simply prefer one large sink over two smaller ones, the extra counter space the Merax offers over a same-width double-basin vanity is the main argument for paying its higher price.

Two people needing the sink area at once makes it a weaker fit, since eclife's 48 inch double-sink vanity covers the same width for $50 less. It's also the wrong size for a powder room or a small guest bathroom, where Tennant Brand's 24 inch Felix fits a footprint the Merax simply can't match. Budget-first buyers should look elsewhere too: both eclife vanities in this comparison undercut the Merax while occupying the same 48 inches of wall space.
